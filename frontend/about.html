Platform Abstract

The Multimodal Imperative in Clinical Decision Support

Clinical diagnostic reasoning rarely relies on a single data stream. A radiologist reading a chest radiograph incorporates clinical history and vital signs; an emergency physician evaluating acute dyspnea relies simultaneously on thoracic imaging, oxygenation saturation levels, and symptom onset timelines.

MediFusion AI investigates the fusion architecture required to combine continuous image representations (512-dim visual embeddings), discrete symptom categorical states (15 binary features), and standardized continuous physiological signals (7 vital measurements) into an integrated decision boundary with attention-weighted gating.

Critical Scientific Disclosure

Dataset Pairing Characteristics

The training corpus utilizes two open-access Kaggle benchmark datasets:

  • 1. Chest X-Ray Images (Pneumonia) by Paul Mooney — 5,856 labeled chest radiographs (1,583 Normal, 4,273 Pneumonia) across standardized train/val/test splits.
  • 2. Symptom-Based Disease Prediction Dataset by MiltonMacGyver — 4,998 structured records covering 15 binary symptoms mapping to Malaria, Pneumonia, and Typhoid.
Honest Data Documentation: These two underlying benchmark datasets do not share patient-level identification numbers. Multimodal fusion in this prototype demonstrates the machine learning fusion architecture (gated attention, cross-modal tokenization, and modality-masking) rather than a clinically validated patient-paired cohort.
